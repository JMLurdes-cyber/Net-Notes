### CONCEPTS
Understanding the [[IEEE]] standards of wireless  networks

### KEY TAKEAWAYS
For a router or a wireless networking device to be approved it must fulfill two obligations: *it must follow the **IEEE** (Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers) standards and have the **Wi-Fi Alliance** trademark*. 

The standard that encompasses wireless networks is the 802.11, like 802.11ax, but newer standards have more marketable names like *"Wi-fi 6"*

#### 4G
These standards are also used for mobile networking cases. Whenever an user gets wi-fi from a provider, the kind of communication is called **LTE** *(Long Term Evolution)* or **4G** technology. There are two kinds, for the moment:

- ***LTE*** (Long Term Evolution): which supports download rates of 150 MB/s
- ***LTE-A*** (Long Term evolution Advanced): which supports download rates of 300 MB/s

#### 5G
This is the newer standard for network providers. The objective is, with this standard, to reach 10 GB/s transfers. That said, common, slower connections are based around 100 / 900 MB/s. 

Thanks to this connections, the bandwidth and clogging become less of an issue, making viable larger, more taxing connections, expanding the amount of tasks managed online without causing major issues.

#### SATELLITE NETWORKING
These are connections used on remote sites where implementing a full system of networking would be far too costly, or simply not worth it.

Since the information has to get to the satellite and then go back to the parting point, it tends to take a bit of time before the information is received by the user, taking up to 250 ms in the past. Nowadays, some providers advertise latency of 40 ms.

These connections are technically weather based, since a rainy or a stormy weather that clogs the communications will cause it to be fully interrupted, and unable to communicate.
